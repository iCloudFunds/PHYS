1. Mechanics, Forces & Motion +

1. Newton's Laws & Equilibrium

  • Newton's 3rd Law: For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. Crucial: The two forces act on DIFFERENT bodies.
  • Equilibrium: When a body is at rest, the sum of all forces is zero. You can use Lami's Theorem or resolve forces vertically and horizontally to find unknown tensions.

2. Circular Motion & Gravitation

  • Horizontal Circle: A girl swinging a stone has constant speed, but changing velocity (because direction changes). Acceleration is towards the centre (a = v²/r).
  • Geostationary Satellites: Orbit the equator with a period of 24 hours. The equation is r³ = GMT²/4π². They are used for communication.
  • Gravitational Field: g = GM/r². Inside a solid sphere (r < R), g is proportional to r. Outside (r > R), g is inversely proportional to r².

3. Work, Energy & Power

  • Work Done: W = Force × distance moved in direction of force. If an electric field moves a charge Q through p.d. V, Work = QV.
  • Falling Bodies: Displacement s = ½gt² (parabola graph). Total energy remains constant.
2. Thermal Physics & Gases +

1. Heat vs Temperature

  • Heat: Total thermal energy (depends on mass).
  • Temperature: Average kinetic energy of particles. Scales: Celsius, Kelvin. Kelvin does NOT depend on the property of the substance.
  • Specific Heat Capacity (c): Energy to raise 1kg by 1°C. Q = mcΔT.
  • Specific Latent Heat (L): Energy to change state (melting/boiling) without temperature change. Q = mL. For example, calculating the mass of ice melted by steam: Heat lost by steam = Heat gained by ice.

2. Thermodynamics

  • First Law: ΔU = Q + W. (Change in internal energy = Heat added + Work done ON the gas).
  • Isothermal: Constant temperature. No change in internal energy. No energy enters or leaves.
  • Adiabatic: No heat enters or leaves (Q = 0). Usually happens fast, or in a highly insulated container.
  • Ideal Gas: PV = nRT. P = (1/3)ρc². Average kinetic energy = (3/2)kT.

3. Heat Transfer

  • Metals have positive temperature coefficient (resistance increases with temperature).
  • Conduction: Heat flows from hot to cold. Rate of heat flow is proportional to temperature gradient and thermal conductivity. In a steady state, the rate of heat flow through composite bars is the same for all sections.
3. Waves, Optics & Superposition +

1. Wave Properties

  • Mechanical Waves: Need a medium to travel. They are longitudinal or transverse. They are not deflected by electric/magnetic fields.
  • Refractive Index (n): n = c/v. From medium 1 to 2: n₁sinθ₁ = n₂sinθ₂.
  • Total Internal Reflection (TIR): Only happens when light goes from a denser to a less dense medium, and the angle of incidence is greater than the critical angle. sin(C) = n₂/n₁.

2. Superposition & Diffraction

  • Interference: Requires coherent sources (same frequency, constant phase difference).
  • Diffraction Grating: d sinθ = nλ. To find the maximum number of images/orders, set θ = 90° and solve for n.
  • Stationary Waves: Formed by two identical waves traveling in opposite directions.
4. Electricity & Magnetism +

1. Circuits & Components

  • Internal Resistance (r): E = I(R + r). Real batteries have internal resistance. To find it, use two sets of readings.
  • Voltmeter Multiplier: To measure a higher voltage, connect a high-value resistor in series with the voltmeter.
  • Ammeter Shunt: To measure higher current, connect a low-value resistor in parallel with the ammeter.

2. Electric Fields

  • Electric Field Strength (E): Inside a charged sphere, E = 0. Outside, E = kQ/r² (inverse square law curve).
  • Electric potential decreases as you move further from a positive charge.

3. Magnetism & Induction

  • Electromagnetic Induction: A changing magnetic flux induces an e.m.f. Faraday's law: E = -N(dΦ/dt).
  • Transformer: Vs/Vp = Ns/Np. Efficiency = (VsIs)/(VpIp). Losses are due to eddy currents (reduced by laminations), hysteresis, and resistance.
5. Modern Physics & Nuclear +

1. Photoelectric Effect

  • Light behaves as a particle (photon). Energy E = hf.
  • Work Function (Φ): Minimum energy needed to eject an electron.
  • Einstein's equation: max KE = hf - Φ. The graph of KE vs f has a gradient of h (Planck's constant) and a y-intercept of -Φ.
  • Classical theory fails because it cannot explain the threshold frequency or why max KE depends on frequency, not intensity.

2. Nuclear Physics

  • Half-life: Time for half the radioactive nuclei to decay. Activity halves every half-life. Decay constant λ = ln(2)/T_half.
  • Mass Defect: When nucleons form a nucleus, mass is lost. Energy released E = mc².
  • Alpha decay: emits alpha particle (2p, 2n). Beta decay: emits electron (from neutron). Gamma: pure energy.

3. Semiconductors

  • Intrinsic: Pure. Number of holes = number of electrons.
  • Extrinsic: Doped. N-type (extra electrons), P-type (extra holes).
  • Zener Diode: Designed to work in reverse breakdown, used for voltage regulation.
6. Practical Skills & Data Analysis +

1. Units & Dimensions

  • Base SI Units: Mass (kg), Length (m), Time (s), Current (A), Temperature (K), Amount (mol), Luminous intensity (cd).
  • Derived Units: Newton (kg m s⁻²), Joule (kg m² s⁻²), Watt (kg m² s⁻³).
  • Homogeneity: Both sides of the equation must have the same base units. However, homogeneity doesn't give you the dimensionless constants (like the ½ in ½mv²).

2. Graphs & Errors

  • Always plot the variables to get a straight line (e.g., for P = P₀e^(-mgh/RT), plot ln(P) against h to get a gradient).
  • Reading errors: Always take measurements from the center of a meniscus or a pointer.
  • For a pendulum, measure time for 20 oscillations and divide by 20 to reduce human reaction error.
